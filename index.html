The terrain of a definition: what is geography?

Definitions of geography printed in the Journal of Geography, raised into a block diagram. Each ridge is one way of answering the question. It stands tall where that answer was given often. Every pin is one definition you can read.

How to read the diagram

Left to right is time, 1902 to 2026. Ridge height is the number of definitions of that kind printed around that year, smoothed over about a year either side. Every decade from the 1900s to the 2020s is represented.

The sorting into ten schools is an interpretation, made by reading each passage. A definition that fits two schools was placed with the idea it leans on hardest. The two ridges at the back, “A way of thinking” and “Many geographies,” were added on 27 November 2026 after a second reading found definitions that name a method or a plurality rather than a subject matter; the pins that moved there are ringed in white.

Quotations were cleaned of scanning errors. The file sometimes lists a passage under the article next to the one it came from, so check the DOI before citing.

The data

Every pin comes from one CSV of passages found by scanning the Journal for sentences that define geography: 1,549 passages with year, author, title, volume, DOI and the surrounding text, including the passages that were read and not pinned. Download the CSV (1.1 MB). A second, smaller CSV holds the 359 passages that state a definition outright, each with a coded rhetorical function and, since 27 November 2026, a school code; the code reads the passage as a whole, so where one passage holds several definitions its pins may sit on different ridges.
